# Starting a game, and matches of several games

## Starting a game

- **Who starts.** In the first game of a match, any agreed method picks the player who chooses who takes the
  first turn. In later games the loser of the previous game chooses; after a drawn game, whoever chose last time
  chooses again (CR 103.1, level 1). At a tournament: in Swiss rounds the winner of a random method chooses, in
  playoffs the higher-ranked player. The choice comes before looking at the hand, and a player who makes none
  plays first. After a game, the loser may wait until after sideboarding to choose (MTR 2.2, level 2).
- **Mulligans.** The London mulligan: draw seven, and for each mulligan shuffle back and draw seven again, then
  put one card on the bottom per mulligan taken (CR 103.5). The first mulligan is free in any multiplayer game
  and in any Brawl game (CR 103.5c, 903.12g). It is not free in a two-player Commander game.
  **Don't follow the MTR's pointer here:** MTR 2.3 sends readers to "rule 103.4" for mulligans and MTR 9.4 to
  "103.4c" for Two-Headed Giant. Those numbers are out of date: in the current CR, 103.4 is starting life totals
  and mulligans are 103.5 (Two-Headed Giant's team mulligan is 103.5d).
- **The first draw.** In a two-player game the starting player skips their first draw; in Two-Headed Giant the
  starting team does; in every other multiplayer game nobody skips it (CR 103.8a-c). A game is multiplayer if
  it *begins* with more than two players (CR 100.1b), so this is settled per game.
- **Before the first turn.** Sideboards and substitute cards are set aside; one companion may be revealed from
  outside the game; commanders go to the command zone (CR 103.2a-c).

## Matches of several games

- **Two players.** A match usually goes until one player has won two games (CR 100.6a). At a tournament the
  organizer may set another number, announced before the event. Drawn games don't count, so a match can run to a
  fourth or fifth game. If the round ends first, the player with more game wins takes the match; equal wins make
  it a draw (MTR 2.1). What happens when time is called: MTR 2.4.
- **Three or more players.** A multiplayer match usually has one game (CR 100.6a). No official rule says who
  starts a later game or how a multiplayer match of several games ends: "the loser of the previous game"
  (CR 103.1) has no single meaning when several players lost. The MTR's only multiplayer structure is
  Two-Headed Giant (MTR section 9).
  - A community answer (level 4): the judges' Multiplayer Addendum (2026-09-27) plays one game by default; for
    longer matches it keeps the seats, draws the first player at random each game, and if time runs out gives
    the match to the player with the most game wins, a tie being a draw for everyone in it.
  - Seat order matters in four-player games (level 4, playgroup.gg data): over 284,950 four-player games the
    first seat won 29.2% and the fourth 21.5%.
- **Each game starts fresh.** Life totals, zones, commander tax and commander damage all reset (CR 903.6, 903.8,
  903.10a).
- **Ending a game.** A player may concede at any time and leaves the game (CR 104.3a). A game is a draw if all
  remaining players lose at once, if a loop of mandatory actions can't be stopped, or if an effect says so
  (CR 104.4a-c). Intentional draws are a tournament matter (CR 104.4i; MTR 2.5: allowed until the result is
  recorded, never in exchange for anything).

## Sideboarding

- **When:** sideboard cards may be used from game 2 on (MTR 3.16). Before each game, each player presents their
  sideboard face down, and the opponent may count it.
- **How many:** Constructed, up to 15 (CR 100.4a, MTR 6.1); Limited, the rest of the pool plus basic lands
  (CR 100.4b, MTR 7.1-7.2). Swaps don't have to be one for one (MTR 3.16).
- **Between matches:** the deck and sideboard return to their registered form before the first game of each
  match (MTR 3.16), except Limited events without decklists that allow continuous construction (MTR 7.3).
- **No sideboard after a pre-game loss:** if a penalty makes a player lose the first game before it begins, or
  the first game is intentionally drawn before any cards are played, neither player may sideboard for the next
  game (MTR 3.16; IPG 1.1 says the same for a Game Loss issued before the match begins).
- **"Outside the game"** means the sideboard in tournament play (MTR 3.16). Commander and Brawl use no
  sideboards (CR 903.5e, 903.12a); in Oathbreaker those abilities don't work at all (Oathbreaker rules 906.13).

## Digital clients, for comparison (not rules)

- **MTG Arena** runs best-of-one and best-of-three queues. Best of one has no sideboarding and draws the opening
  hand with a "smoothing" algorithm (community sources). Brawl: see `brawl.md`.
- **Magic Online** two-player queues and leagues play matches to two wins; its Duel Commander plays three games
  at 20 life (MTGO announcement, 2024-10-07).
